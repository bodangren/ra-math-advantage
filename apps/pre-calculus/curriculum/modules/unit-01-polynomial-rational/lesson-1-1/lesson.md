## Vocabulary

### Section: text

- **Independent variable**: The variable that represents the input value. It is often represented by $x$ or $t$.
- **Dependent variable**: The variable that represents the output value. Its value depends on the input. It is often represented by $y$ or $f(x)$.
- **Domain**: The domain is the set of input values.
- **Range**: The range is the set of output values.
- **Covariation**: A description of how two quantities change together. Use directional language: "As $x$ increases from $a$ to $b$, $y$ increases, decreases, or remains constant."
- **Increasing**: A function is increasing on an interval if the output values rise as the input values move from left to right.
- **Decreasing**: A function is decreasing on an interval if the output values fall as the input values move from left to right.
- **Constant**: A function is constant on an interval if the output values stay the same as the input values change.

A function maps each input to exactly one output. The input values form the **domain**. The output values form the **range**.

## Learn

### Section: text

A finite table gives only sampled values. It does not prove behavior between inputs. When a question asks you to infer behavior between table entries, state the model assumption.

A function can model the relationship between two quantities that change together. A function can be represented in four ways: **verbally**, **graphically**, **numerically** (tables), and **analytically** (equations).

The behavior of a function is described by identifying where it is increasing, decreasing, or constant — and by describing the *rate* at which it is changing.

**Critical distinction**: "The function is increasing" describes the *direction* of the output — it means $y$ values are getting larger. This is NOT the same as "$y$ is positive." A function can be increasing while being negative. For example, $f(x) = x$ is increasing on $[-5, -1]$ even though all output values are negative.

When describing behavior, always use intervals written with the **input variable**: "f is increasing on $[a, b]$," not "f is increasing from $f(a)$ to $f(b)$."

Use this sentence frame: "As $x$ increases from ___ to ___, $y$ increases, decreases, or remains constant by approximately ___."

## Worked Examples from the original source

### Section: text

**Example 1:** An empty vase has a flat narrow base, a wide rounded body, and a short narrow neck. It fills with water at a constant volume rate. Time is the independent variable. Water height is the dependent variable. Select the possible height-versus-time graph.

- Option A is a straight increasing line.
- Option B increases slowly, then quickly, and then slowly.
- Option C increases, decreases, and then increases.
- Option D always increases. It starts steep, becomes less steep through the wide body, and becomes steeper through the narrow neck.

**Solution:** Select graph D. The height always increases. It rises quickly in narrow parts of the vase and slowly in wide parts.

**Example 2:** The graph of $f$ on $0 \le x \le 9$ uses straight segments from $(0,-2)$ to $(3,4)$, from $(3,4)$ to $(7,1)$, and from $(7,1)$ to $(9,3)$. Find all intervals where $f$ increases.

**Solution:** The graph increases from 0 to 3 and from 7 to 9. It decreases from 3 to 7. It has no constant segment.

**Example 3:** The graph of $h$ is a smooth periodic curve with dashed midline $h=0$. It has a maximum at $F(-6,4)$, crosses the midline at $G(-3,0)$, has a minimum at $J(0,-4)$, crosses the midline at $K(3,0)$, and has a maximum at $P(6,4)$. The arc from F to G is concave down. Let $t_1$ and $t_2$ be the input coordinates of F and G. Classify $h$ on $(t_1,t_2)$. Choose positive and increasing, positive and decreasing, negative and increasing, or negative and decreasing. Then describe how its rate changes.

**Solution:** The function is positive and decreasing between F and G. Its rate of change decreases because the slope becomes more negative.

**Example 4:** A second smooth periodic graph of $h$ has dashed midline $h=-4$. It has a maximum at $F(0,-1)$, crosses the midline at $G(3,-4)$, has a minimum at $J(6,-7)$, crosses the midline at $K(9,-4)$, and has a maximum at $P(12,-1)$. The arc from K to P is concave down. Let $t_1$ and $t_2$ be the input coordinates of K and P. Classify $h$ on $(t_1,t_2)$. Use the same four sign-and-direction choices as Example 3. Then describe how its rate changes.

**Solution:** The function is negative and increasing between K and P. Its positive rate decreases as the graph approaches P.

Examples 5 through 8 use one graph. The graph is a smooth curve through A(0,-2), B(2,0), C(4,2), D(7,-1), E(8,-2), and F(9,-1). It rises from A through B to the local maximum at C. It falls from C through D to the local minimum at E. It then rises to F. The curve is concave up from A to B, concave down from B through C to D, and concave up from D through E to F.

**Example 5:** On which interval is $k$ negative and decreasing? The choices are A to B, B to C, D to E, and E to F.

**Solution:** Choice C, from D to E.

**Example 6:** Which statement about the rate of change of $k$ is true?

- (A) The rate of change is negative from A to B.
- (B) The rate of change is negative from B to C.
- (C) The rate of change is positive from D to E.
- (D) The rate of change is positive from E to F.

**Solution:** Choice D. The rate of change is positive from E to F.

**Example 7:** Which statement about the rate of change of $k$ is true?

- (A) The rate of change is decreasing from A to B.
- (B) The rate of change is increasing from B to C.
- (C) The rate of change is increasing from D to E.
- (D) The rate of change is decreasing from E to F.

**Solution:** Choice C. The rate of change increases from D to E. The downward slope becomes less negative.

**Example 8:** On which interval is $k$ increasing and concave down? The choices are A to B, B to C, C to D, and D to E.

**Solution:** Choice B, from B to C.

**Example 9:** A piecewise graph of $f$ on $-5 \le x \le 5$ uses straight segments from $(-5,-2)$ to $(-1,2)$, from $(-1,2)$ to $(2,0)$, and from $(2,0)$ to $(5,2)$. Find where $f$ decreases.

**Solution:** The function decreases for $-1 < x < 2$.

**Example 10:** Use the same graph. Find where $f$ is negative and increasing.

**Solution:** The function is negative and increasing for $-5 < x < -3$. The graph has value zero at $x=-3$, so $-3$ is excluded.

**Example 11:** A smooth graph of $g$ on $-5 \le x \le 5$ has a local maximum near $x=-3$, a marked change in concavity at $A(-1,0)$, and a local minimum near $x=1$. It is concave down for $-5 < x < -1$ and concave up for $-1 < x < 5$. Find where $g$ decreases and is concave up.

**Solution:** The function decreases and is concave up for $-1 < x < 1$.

**Example 12:** Use the same graph and its stated concavity arcs. Find where the rate of change is positive and decreasing.

**Solution:** The rate of change is positive and decreasing for $-5 < x < -3$. The slope is zero at the local maximum, so $-3$ is excluded.

## Authored Worked Examples

### Section: text

**Authored worked example A:** The table shows sampled values of function $g$:

| x | -2 | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| g(x) | 11 | 7 | 3 | 3 | 7 |

Compare each pair of consecutive displayed values. Do not infer behavior between the displayed inputs.

**Solution:**
- From $x=-2$ to $x=0$, $g(x)$ decreases by 4, from 11 to 7.
- From $x=0$ to $x=2$, $g(x)$ decreases by 4, from 7 to 3.
- From $x=2$ to $x=4$, the displayed values remain 3.
- From $x=4$ to $x=6$, $g(x)$ increases by 4, from 3 to 7.

**Authored worked example B:** A piecewise linear function has three segments. On $[-3,-1]$, the graph rises. On $[-1,3]$, the graph falls. On $[3,5]$, the graph is horizontal.

**Solution:**
- The function is increasing on $[-3,-1]$.
- The function is decreasing on $[-1,3]$.
- The function is constant on $[3,5]$.
